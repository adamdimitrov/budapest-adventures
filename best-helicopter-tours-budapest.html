A gyrocopter is the budget way to fly over Budapest, and arguably the more thrilling one. From €149 per person you sit in an open or closed cockpit next to a licensed pilot, and on the open version there is no window between you and the view.

The three routes are the reason to look at it. It suits adventurous travellers flying on their own; because it is priced per person, a full helicopter gets closer in price per seat as your group grows.

The big practical advantage is the airfield. Hármashatárhegy is on the Buda hills in District II, inside the city, and transport from the base of the hill up to the airfield is included. Compare that with Budaörs for #1 and #3 or Budakeszi for the gyrocopter. From the air you see the Parliament, Chain Bridge and Buda Castle, with the Buda Mountains around you.

The 10-minute flight works out at about €11 a minute; the 30-minute flight costs more, so compare both options when you book if you want longer air time. Watch the limits: over-12s only and a 91 kg maximum. It is a new listing, so there are no reviews yet.

  • Couple or group of 3, best value: #1 Aeroglobe, 20 minutes for €550.
  • Family or group of 4: #2 Balaton Fly (transfer and sparkling wine included) or #3 Heli Hungary for a longer slot.
  • You want the Danube Bend: #3 Heli Hungary by helicopter, or #4 gyrocopter's 60–75 minute route on your own.
  • Special occasion: #2 for the fizz, or #3 with a sunset flight on request.
  • Solo and adventurous: #4 gyrocopter for open-cockpit thrills, #5 hang-glider for the lowest price and a take-off inside the city.
  • Wheelchair user: #3 is the only flight listed as wheelchair-accessible.

Where the Flights Take Off (and How to Get There)

No flight leaves from the riverbank. Plan the journey before you book:

  • Budaörs Airport (LHBS), Kőérberki út 36/A: #1 and #3. On the south-western edge of Budapest; no transfer, so take a taxi or drive.
  • Heliport via Batthyány tér: #2. The return transfer is included, so this is the easiest from a central hotel.
  • Farkashegy Airfield, Budakeszi: #4. In the hills west of Buda, outside the city limits; no transfer.
  • Hármashatárhegy, Cenk utca 3 (District II): #5. Inside the city on the Buda hills; transport from the base of the hill to the airfield is included.

Weather, Limits and What to Bring

Weather: all flights depend on it. Aeroglobe (#1) states that a weather cancellation gets a full refund, and all five come with free cancellation up to 24 hours before, so book for the start of your trip and keep a spare day to rebook. The gyrocopter flies year-round.

Weight and age limits: the hang-glider takes passengers aged 12+ up to 91 kg; the gyrocopter up to 118 kg. The gyrocopter is not for people afraid of heights or with vertigo, and #1 is not suitable for people with vertigo either. On #2, children get noise-cancelling earplugs.

What to bring: sunglasses, a camera and comfortable clothes (#1). Drones are not allowed on #1, loose clothing is not allowed on the gyrocopter, and alcohol is not allowed before the hang-glider flight.

Time to allow: #2's 15 minutes in the air is a 1.5–2 hour outing with the transfer and safety briefing. For #1, #3 and #4, add your own travel time to Budaörs or Budakeszi.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a helicopter tour in Budapest?

From €550 per group (up to 3 passengers, 20 minutes) to €980 (up to 4 passengers, 1-hour slot). Full, that is €183–245 a seat. A 15-minute flight for up to 4 with transfer costs €790.

Is a Budapest helicopter tour priced per person?

No. All three helicopter flights here are priced per group: up to 3 passengers on #1 and up to 4 on #2 and #3. The gyrocopter (€149) and powered hang-glider (€109.79) are per person.

Where do helicopter tours in Budapest take off?

#1 and #3 fly from Budaörs Airport on the city's south-western edge (Kőérberki út 36/A). #2 includes a transfer from Batthyány tér to the heliport. The gyrocopter flies from Farkashegy in Budakeszi, the hang-glider from Hármashatárhegy in District II.

Can I fly over the Danube Bend from Budapest?

Yes. Heli Hungary's 1-hour helicopter flight offers a Visegrád and Danube Bend route for up to 4, and the gyrocopter has a 60–75 minute Danube Bend route, priced per person.

What is the cheapest scenic flight in Budapest?

The powered hang-glider at €109.79 for 10 minutes, taking off from Hármashatárhegy inside the city. The gyrocopter starts at €149 per person, with routes from 25–30 minutes.

What will I see on a Budapest helicopter flight?

The city routes cover the Parliament, Buda Castle and the Royal Palace, the Chain Bridge and Elizabeth Bridge, Gellért Hill with the Citadel, Margaret Island and, depending on the flight, St Stephen's Basilica, Heroes' Square or the Buda Hills. Read up on the Parliament before you fly over it.
