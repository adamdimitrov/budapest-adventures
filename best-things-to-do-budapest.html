25 Best Things to Do in Budapest in 2026 (Local's Guide)

By Adam Dimitrov · Founder & Local Budapest Guide
Top 10 visited in person • Prices and booking ratings checked November 2026
Last updated: Nov 21, 2026

Most Budapest lists hand you 40 sights in no particular order. With two or three days, what you need to know is which ten to do first, which ones are free, and which to keep for a rainy afternoon or your last evening.

So here are the 25 best things to do in Budapest, ranked. The top 10 are the stops I would send a friend to first, from the Danube lit up after dark to Széchenyi's outdoor pools; numbers 11 to 25 add the Jewish Quarter, the castle caves, Gellért Hill, communist history and a Danube Bend day trip. Every card shows the cost, the time it takes and the one ticket worth booking, and 10 of the 25 are free. Filter below, or jump to the 1, 2 and 3-day plans.

✓ Pick it for your first evening: after dark the Parliament, Buda Castle and the bridges are lit up and reflected in the river. ✕ Skip it if you'd rather stay on land: the hop-on hop-off buses cover both banks, and the two big ones include a boat ride.
❓ 5 questions travellers ask ▾
Do I need to book ahead?
No, you can book on the same day too. Evening sailings in December and on weekends can sell out, though, and cancellation is free up to 24 hours before, so booking early costs nothing.
Day or evening?
Evening: 17 of 64 recent guests name the lit-up city as the highlight. The Parliament's lights go off at 22:00.
Will I get a good seat?
Seats are not assigned, so arrive 15 to 20 minutes early. A few guests felt the boat was busy.
What if my headset doesn't work?
Guests say there are plenty of spare headsets on board.
Why does it cost more?
Guests call it well organised and a bit more upmarket than other boats: headsets in 30 languages, a covered dock with restrooms and a real welcome drink.

🏆 #1 Must-Do Budapest Local's Guide

1. Go on a River Cruise on the Danube

▾
📍 Area: Danube, between Buda & Pest
💶 Cost: Paid ticket
⏱ Time needed: About 1 hour
🕒 Tip: After dark, when the city is lit up
➡️ Compare the 10 best Danube cruises

Location: Danube Promenade, Pest

If it’s your first time in Budapest, I recommend going on a sightseeing river cruise. This is one of the best ways to get an overview of the city and see some of the main sights.

Prefer to stay on land? The best hop-on hop-off bus tickets cover both banks in one loop, and the two big operators include a Danube boat ride in the price.

You’ll pass by landmarks like the Hungarian Parliament Building, Buda Castle, and Fisherman’s Bastion.

I did several evening cruises, and it was magical seeing the lights reflecting on the river. Many cruises offer dinner or live music, but even a basic sightseeing cruise is enough to appreciate the beauty of Budapest from the water.

▾

💶 Cost: Paid ticket
⏱ Time needed: 2–3 hours

Location: City Park, Pest

Visiting a thermal bath is a must in Budapest, and the Széchenyi Thermal Bath is the biggest and one of the best with its Neo-Baroque and Neo-Renaissance architecture.

There are 18 pools, both indoor and outdoor, fed by natural hot springs. After a day of sightseeing, soaking in the warm water is amazing.

I recommend trying the outdoor pools, especially watching locals play chess while relaxing in the bath. This spot is great for unwinding and experiencing a part of Budapest’s culture.

3. Take a Guided Tour in the Budapest Parliament

▾
📍 Area: Kossuth Square, Pest
💶 Cost: Paid guided tour (book ahead)
⏱ Time needed: About 45 minutes
🕒 Tip: Book tickets in advance, tours sell out
➡️ Parliament tickets & tour guide

Location: Pest Side, near the Danube River

The Hungarian Parliament Building is stunning both inside and out. The building is enormous and impressive, with intricate details in its neo-Gothic architecture.

Joining a guided tour would be worth it since you will learn about Hungary’s history and see the Hungarian Crown Jewels up close.

The marble halls and gold decorations make this one of the most beautiful government buildings I’ve ever visited.

Make sure to include this in your list of top things to do in Budapest and book your tickets ahead of time, as tours often sell out.

Fisherman’s Bastion is one of my favorite spots in Budapest for panoramic views. The architecture is unique, almost like something from a fairytale, with its seven towers representing the seven Magyar tribes.

I recommend visiting early in the morning or in the evening when it’s less crowded, allowing you to fully appreciate the breathtaking scenery and take some fantastic photos.

#6 Top Sight Budapest Local's Guide

6. Visit St. Stephen's Basilica

▾
📍 Area: District V, Pest
💶 Cost: Paid entry & dome
⏱ Time needed: About 1 hour
🕒 Tip: Climb the dome for a 360° view

Location: District V, Pest

St. Stephen's Basilica is a must-see landmark. Named after the first king of Hungary, St. Stephen, this cathedral is filled with incredible details, from the high domed ceiling to the elaborate frescoes and gold accents.

The Basilica is also home to Hungary's most sacred relic, St. Stephen’s mummified right hand.

Climbing to the top of the dome and the 360-degree view of the city is worth every one of the 364 steps. If you have the time, I suggest attending one of the concerts often held inside, the acoustics are wonderful.

7. Walk Through the Chain Bridge

▾
📍 Area: Between Buda and Pest
💶 Cost: Free
⏱ Time needed: 15–20 minutes
🕒 Tip: At night, when it is lit up
➡️ See it from the water: night cruises

Location: Between Buda and Pest

The Chain Bridge was the first permanent bridge to link Buda and Pest, and it remains a symbol of unity in the city. Walking across this bridge is one of those quintessential things to do in Budapest.

The stone lions guarding the bridge, the grandeur of its iron chains, and the spectacular views of Gellért Hill and both riverbanks are worth stopping for.

I recommend taking this walk at night when the bridge itself is lit up, and you can see both sides of the city glowing in the dark. It’s a short walk but filled with iconic views that shouldn’t be missed.

#15 Budapest Local's Guide

15. Pause at the Shoes on the Danube Bank

▾
📍 Area: Danube bank by the Parliament, Pest
💶 Cost: Free
⏱ Time needed: 15–20 minutes
🕒 Tip: Walk here from the Parliament
➡️ Compare walking tours

Location: Danube bank by the Parliament, Pest

Along the Pest embankment, a few minutes south of the Parliament, 60 pairs of iron shoes stand at the water's edge. The memorial, by film director Can Togay and sculptor Gyula Pauer, was unveiled in 2005 for the people shot into the Danube by Arrow Cross militiamen in 1944 and 1945, most of them Jews, who were made to take off their shoes first.

It is free, open at any hour and takes a few minutes, but it is the stop many visitors remember longest. Treat it as a memorial, not a photo set.

To see it with context, the 2-hour St. Stephen's Basilica and Shoes Memorial tour starts inside the Basilica, crosses to the Chain Bridge, spends 15 minutes at the memorial and ends at the Parliament, from €40.

Location: Döbrentei tér 9, Buda

Where Széchenyi is big and yellow, Rudas is small and old: its thermal pool sits under a 16th-century Ottoman dome, and a rooftop hot tub looks over the Danube.

The official all-zones day ticket covers the Turkish bath, the wellness area, the saunas and the rooftop: 12,000 HUF at the desk Monday to Thursday, 15,000 HUF Friday to Sunday. Rudas also opens for night bathing on Friday and Saturday nights.

Visitors must be 14 or older.

Location: Between Margaret and Árpád bridges

Margaret Island is a car-free park in the middle of the Danube, where locals run the rubber track that circles it (about 5 km). On the way you pass the musical fountain, the Japanese garden and the old water tower.

In summer, the Palatinus Bath on the island is the best family pool day in the city, with a wave pool and slides, from €27. In winter it closes at 16:00 and many outdoor pools are shut.

🕒 Tip: Pair it with the House of Terror

After 1989, Budapest's communist statues were moved to Memento Park on the city's edge: Lenin, Red Army soldiers, workers' heroes and a replica of the grandstand with Stalin's boots.

The Memento Park ticket is €9.50 with the film show and exhibitions, or €24 with a guided tour. It is about 40 minutes each way by public transport, so plan half a day.

On a short trip, the House of Terror (#16) covers the same era in the centre.

Budapest Things to Do: FAQ

How many days do you need in Budapest?

Two to three days is enough for the top 10 on this list and a few of the next 15. Spend one day in Buda (Castle District and Fisherman's Bastion), one in Pest (Parliament, St. Stephen's Basilica and the Jewish Quarter), and an evening on a Danube cruise. Add a day for a slow morning at Széchenyi Bath and City Park. To put it all into days, follow my 3-day Budapest itinerary. For the practical side, see the guides to getting from the airport to the city center and the best hostels in Budapest.

With a fourth day, get out of the city: the Danube Bend towns of Szentendre, Visegrád and Esztergom are the classic choice. Our best day trips from Budapest compares 13 of them on time at the destination vs on the road.

What are the best free things to do in Budapest?

Walking the Castle District, the lower terraces of Fisherman's Bastion, crossing the Chain Bridge at night, the grounds of Vajdahunyad Castle in City Park, and stepping into Szimpla Kert all cost nothing. So do the Shoes on the Danube Bank, Heroes' Square, the Great Market Hall, the Gellért Hill lookout and Margaret Island.

What is the best thing to do in Budapest at night?

A Danube river cruise after dark is the highlight: Parliament, Buda Castle and the bridges are all illuminated. Follow it with a walk over the lit-up Chain Bridge or a drink in a ruin bar such as Szimpla Kert (or join a ruin bar pub crawl). See our best night cruises in Budapest. Visiting in late autumn or winter? See things to do in Budapest in November and December. On 31 December, book a New Year's Eve cruise early: the good boats sell out weeks in advance. Until late December, end the evening at the Budapest Christmas markets by the Basilica and on Vörösmarty tér.

Do I need to book the Budapest Parliament tour in advance?

Yes. Guided tours of the Hungarian Parliament often sell out, especially in high season, so book your ticket a few days ahead. Our Parliament visitor guide covers tickets and tour times.

Which thermal bath should I visit first in Budapest?

For a first visit, Széchenyi is the classic choice: it is the largest bath, with 18 indoor and outdoor pools in City Park. On Saturday nights it hosts Sparty, the thermal bath pool party. Compare it with Rudas and the others in our Budapest thermal baths guide.

Is the Budapest Card worth it?

It depends on how many paid attractions and public transport rides you will use. Plug your plans into our Budapest Card calculator to see whether it saves you money.

What should I not miss in Budapest?

A Danube cruise after dark, a soak in Széchenyi or Rudas, the Parliament tour and the view from Fisherman's Bastion or Gellért Hill. If you only have one day, follow the one-day plan above.

What can I do in Budapest when it rains?

Go indoors: the thermal baths, the Parliament, the Buda Castle caves, the House of Terror, the Opera House tour, the Great Market Hall or a chimney cake workshop. Use the "Indoor / rainy day" filter above to see all of them.

What is there to do in Budapest in winter?

The outdoor thermal pools are at their best in the cold, the Christmas markets open in late November, and the river cruises run all year. See Budapest in December.

Is Budapest good for kids?

Yes. The castle caves take children from 3, Palatinus on Margaret Island has a wave pool and slides in summer, and the chimney cake workshop is an easy hour. Our Budapest with kids guide has more.
