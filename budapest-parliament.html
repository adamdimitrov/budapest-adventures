Budapest Parliament Tour 2026 (Tickets, Prices & Tips)

By Adam Dimitrov · Founder & Local Budapest Guide
Prices and hours from the Office of the National Assembly • Sources
Last updated: Nov 13, 2026

The Hungarian Parliament is the building on every Budapest postcard, and more than 700,000 people take the official tour each year. You can only go inside on a 45-minute guided tour, and tickets for English slots often sell out.

Short version: book on the official site, jegymester.hu, a few days ahead. Adults pay HUF 7,000 (EU/EEA) or HUF 14,000 (everyone else) in 2026. Below is everything I wish I had known before my first visit.

Budapest Parliament ticket prices 2026

The price depends on two things: your citizenship and your age. Hungarians and citizens of the European Economic Area (the EU plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway) pay the reduced rate. Visitors from the UK, the US, Switzerland and everywhere else pay the full rate.

  • Bring proof. EU/EEA visitors show an ID card or passport. Students need a Hungarian or international (ISIC) student card. Staff can check at the door.

How to book (and avoid sell-outs)

The first time I went, I just turned up. Every English tour was sold out by late morning. Learn from my mistake:

  1. Book online on jegymester.hu. It is the ticket system run for the Office of the National Assembly, so you pay the official price. Pick the date, the language and the time slot.
  2. Book early in high season. From May to September, and on weekends all year, English slots go days ahead. In winter a day or two is usually enough.
  3. Same-day tickets are a gamble. The Visitor Centre ticket office opens at 8:00 and sells whatever is left, by card or cash in forints.
  4. Watch out for resellers. Tour sites and look-alike "Parliament" websites sell the same audioguide tour for more. If the price is not HUF 7,000 or HUF 14,000 plus a small fee, you are not on the official site.

This is a working parliament, so tours stop for plenary sittings, state visits and protocol events. Closures are posted on the ticket site, sometimes only a few days ahead. If your tour is cancelled, you get your money back.

National holidays (15 March, 20 August, 23 October) bring ceremonies on Kossuth Square and changed schedules. Check the booking page the day before you go.

The guided tour: languages and route

Every tour lasts 45 minutes and runs at a fixed time slot. You choose the language when you book:

  • Live guide or audioguide: Hungarian, English, French, German, Italian and Spanish.
  • Audioguide only (mixed groups): Russian, Polish, Slovak, Croatian, Hebrew, Romanian, Serbian, Ukrainian, Korean, Portuguese, Arabic, Slovenian, Czech, Chinese, Japanese, Bulgarian, Turkish, Dutch, Greek and Vietnamese.

The route takes you through the most beautiful parts of the building:

  • The Main Staircase, a riot of gold leaf, frescoes and red carpet.
  • The Dome Hall, where the Holy Crown of Hungary is kept under armed guard. No photos here.
  • The Chamber of the former Upper House (Chamber of Peers) and its lounge, with the famous numbered cigar holders on the window sills.

While you wait, the Visitor Centre has a café, a gift shop and a free exhibition on 1,000 years of Hungarian legislation. Use the toilets before your tour starts.

Security check and what to bring

You enter through an airport-style security check, so arrive 15 minutes before your slot. Latecomers may be turned away.

  • Bring: your ticket, your ID or passport (for EU/EEA prices) and your student card if you bought a student ticket.
  • Leave behind: knives, sharp objects and large bags. Small (0.5 litre) plastic water bottles are fine.
  • Accessibility: the Visitor Centre and the tour are wheelchair accessible. Email tourist.office@parlament.hu before your visit so staff can help.
  • Guide dogs are allowed. Other pets are not.

How to get there

The Parliament sits on the Pest bank of the Danube, a 15-minute walk from the city centre. Kossuth Square is car-free and there is no visitor parking, so use public transport:

  • Metro M2 (red line) to Kossuth Lajos tér. The station exit is on the square.
  • Tram 2 along the Danube. It is one of the most scenic tram rides in Europe and stops right below the building.
  • Trolleybus 70 or 78 to Kossuth Lajos tér.

Single tickets and travel passes are sold in the BudapestGo app. If you plan a busy few days, compare the price of a pass with the Budapest Card, which includes unlimited public transport.

History and architecture

Buda, Pest and Óbuda merged into Budapest in 1873, and the new capital wanted a parliament to match its ambitions. An international design competition was won by Imre Steindl, and building work started in 1885.

The Parliament was inaugurated in 1896, the 1,000th anniversary of the Hungarian state, and completed in 1904. Steindl went blind before it was finished and died in 1902, two years before completion.

  • Style: Gothic Revival facades and spires on a Baroque-style symmetrical floor plan, with a Renaissance Revival dome.
  • Size: 268 metres long, with 691 rooms. It is the largest building in Hungary.
  • Height: the dome is 96 metres tall, a nod to the year 896, when the Magyars settled the Carpathian Basin. St Stephen's Basilica is the same height, and no building in the centre may be taller.
  • The Holy Crown of Hungary has been kept in the Dome Hall since 2000.

Best views of the Parliament

The tour shows you the inside. For the classic photo, you need to step back:

  • Batthyány tér on the Buda bank, directly opposite. Best at sunset.
  • Fisherman's Bastion and the Buda Castle walls for the view from above.
  • From the Danube after dark. The building is lit up every evening, and the best way to see it is from a boat. I compare the options in my guides to the best Budapest river cruises and the best dinner cruises.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a Budapest Parliament ticket in 2026?

From 1 January 2026 an adult ticket costs HUF 7,000 for EU/EEA citizens and HUF 14,000 for everyone else. Students aged 6 to 24 pay half (HUF 3,500 or HUF 7,000) with a valid student card. Children under 6 go in free.

Can you visit the Hungarian Parliament without a tour?

No. You can only go inside on the official 45-minute tour, with a live guide or an audioguide. The square, the riverside and the free museum in the Visitor Centre are open to everyone.

How long is the Parliament tour?

About 45 minutes. Add 15 minutes for the security check, which you go through before your slot.

Do Parliament tickets sell out?

Yes, especially in summer, at weekends and for English tours. Book online a few days to a few weeks ahead. The Visitor Centre only sells a limited number of same-day tickets.

Where do I buy official Parliament tickets?

On jegymester.hu, the ticket system run for the Office of the National Assembly, or at the Visitor Centre ticket office. Other sites sell the same tour at a higher price.

Can I take photos inside the Parliament?

Yes, in most rooms. Photos and videos are not allowed in the Dome Hall, where the Holy Crown is guarded.

What are the Parliament opening hours?

Daily 8:00 to 18:00 from 1 April to 31 October, and 8:00 to 16:00 from 1 November to 31 March. The building closes for parliamentary sittings and state events, sometimes at short notice.
