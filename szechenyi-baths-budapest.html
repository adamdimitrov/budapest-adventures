Which Széchenyi Ticket Should You Book?

  • Cheapest booked ticket, early riser: Morning Pass (€39 with locker, entry 7:00 to 9:00, Mon to Thu).
  • A normal day at the bath: Weekday Pass (€46 with locker, €50 with a private cabin).
  • Saturday night out: Sparty (from €73, 21:30 to 2:00, 18+, non-refundable).
  • Friends, a birthday or a stag weekend: Beer Spa combo (€125, bath day ticket plus 45 minutes in a beer tub with your own tap).
  • A slow, pampered day with nothing to pack: Palm House VIP Pass (€110, robe, towel, cabin, lounge and a cocktail).
  • Weekend or holiday afternoon, no patience for queues: Fast Track with locker (from €59, separate entrance).

Széchenyi Desk Price vs Online Ticket

At the desk, a 2026 locker ticket costs 13,200 HUF Monday to Thursday and 14,800 HUF Friday to Sunday (15,800 HUF in peak periods). Entry before 9:00 is 10,500 HUF on weekdays and 11,800 HUF at weekends, and a cabin adds 1,000 HUF.

Online you pay a little more (€46 for the weekday locker ticket), and in return you get free cancellation up to 24 hours before, pay-later booking and a QR code that goes straight to the turnstile machines instead of the cashier queue. If you are already standing outside on a quiet weekday, the desk is cheaper. If you are planning ahead, or going at the weekend, book.

Sparty: Széchenyi's Saturday Night Pool Party

Every Saturday from 21:30 to 2:00, the outdoor pools turn into a club: DJs, lasers, fire shows and circus acts around 34 to 38 °C water. Tickets start at €73 with a locker; the €100 premium ticket adds two drink coupons, which buy roughly one cocktail. It is 18+ only and non-refundable, and the bars take only the cash-loaded SpartyPay card.

Széchenyi Baths: Know Before You Go

Opening Hours

Monday to Thursday 7:00 to 20:00, Friday 7:00 to 22:00, Saturday and Sunday 8:00 to 20:00. On 8 December 2026 the bath closes at 16:00; from 18 December 2026 to 6 January 2027 it opens at 8:00, and on 25 and 26 December and 1 January at 10:00.

Best Time to Go

Weekday mornings before 10:00 are the quietest, and the €39 Morning Pass makes them the cheapest too. Late morning to late afternoon, and all weekend, the outdoor pools get crowded. Friday evening, when the bath stays open until 22:00, is the calm way to see the pools steaming in the dark.

Locker or Cabin?

A locker sits in a shared, gender-separated changing room and opens with your wristband. A cabin is a lockable booth for one or two people where you change and leave everything. It costs €4 more online or 1,000 HUF more at the desk: worth it for couples and anyone with a bag.

What to Bring

Swimsuit, towel and flip-flops (no ticket except the Palm House pass includes a towel). If you forget them, the desk sells a towel for 6,900 HUF, slippers for 4,200 HUF and a swim cap for 2,100 HUF. A swim cap is needed only in the lap pool.

Getting There

Take metro M1, the yellow line from Vörösmarty tér, to Széchenyi fürdő. Day tickets enter on the swimming-pool side at Állatkerti körút 9-11; the Beer Spa reception is through the main entrance on Kós Károly sétány.

Age Limits

The booked day tickets are not suitable for children under 14, and Sparty and the Beer Spa are 18+. For a family day with younger kids, see Palatinus in our thermal baths guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a Széchenyi Baths ticket?

At the desk in 2026, a locker ticket is 13,200 HUF Monday to Thursday and 14,800 HUF Friday to Sunday. Booked online, the Morning Pass is €39 (entry 7:00 to 9:00), the Weekday Pass €46 with a locker and €50 with a private cabin.

Should I book Széchenyi tickets in advance?

For weekends, holidays and Sparty, yes. A booked ticket skips the cashier queue, and the day tickets can be cancelled for free up to 24 hours before. On a quiet weekday the desk is cheaper.

What is the difference between a locker and a cabin at Széchenyi?

A locker is in a shared, gender-separated changing room. A cabin is a private lockable booth for one or two people. The cabin costs €4 more online or 1,000 HUF more at the desk.

What time is Sparty and how much does it cost?

Sparty runs every Saturday from 21:30 to 2:00, with last entry at 1:00. Tickets start at €73 with a locker, or €100 with two drink coupons. It is 18+ and non-refundable.

What are Széchenyi's opening hours?

Monday to Thursday 7:00 to 20:00, Friday 7:00 to 22:00, Saturday and Sunday 8:00 to 20:00. Holiday hours differ in December and on 1 January.

Do I need to bring a towel to Széchenyi?

Yes. Towels and flip-flops are not included in standard tickets. The desk sells a towel for 6,900 HUF and slippers for 4,200 HUF; the Palm House pass includes a robe, towel and slippers.

Is Széchenyi or Rudas better?

Széchenyi for big outdoor pools, Friday evenings and Sparty. Rudas for an Ottoman Turkish bath and a rooftop pool over the Danube, plus weekend night bathing.
