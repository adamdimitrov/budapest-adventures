Budapest Itinerary: The 3-Day Plan a Local Would Follow (2 and 4-Day Versions Too)

By Adam Dimitrov, Founder & Budapest Local
Day-by-Day Plan • 8 Bookings Compared • 3 Personally Tested
Last updated: Nov 20, 2026

Most Budapest itineraries get the order wrong. They send you up Castle Hill at midday, when it is busiest, and into Széchenyi in the afternoon, when the same locker ticket costs €46 instead of €39 before 9:00.

I live here, so this plan is built around the hours: Pest and the Parliament on day 1, ending with a cruise after dark; Buda on day 2, with Castle Hill at sunset and the ruin bars after; the baths at opening on day 3. Everything worth booking comes to about €190 per person, and below each day you get the times, the walks between stops and what to skip.

Only have a weekend? Use the 2-day version. Got a fourth day? Add the Danube Bend.

Per person, for the bookings in this plan only (no food, hotel or transport). Total: about €190 for 3 days.

Day 1: Pest, the Parliament and the River After Dark

Morning. Be at the Parliament visitor centre 15 minutes before a 9:00 or 10:00 tour. It lasts 45 minutes. Walk round to the riverside behind it for the view across to Buda.

Late morning. About 15 minutes on foot through Szabadság tér brings you to St Stephen's Basilica. Climb the dome for the 360° view, then have lunch in the streets around it.

Afternoon. Walk down to the river and along the Pest embankment to the Chain Bridge. Leave the castle for tomorrow; today you are looking at it.

Evening. From Dock 7 at Vigadó tér, take the one-hour night cruise. You pass the Parliament lit up, then Buda Castle and the bridges. It is the best hour of the trip.

Day 2: Market, Castle Hill and the Ruin Bars

Morning. Go hungry to the Central Market Hall by Liberty Bridge for the market-to-tavern food tour: 3 hours, 12+ tastings and a wine tasting. It covers lunch.

Afternoon. Bus 16 from Deák Ferenc tér crosses the Chain Bridge and climbs Castle Hill. Be at Trinity Square for the 14:00 castle walk: the Royal Palace, Matthias Church and the Fisherman's Bastion in 2 hours.

Sunset. Stay on the Fisherman's Bastion as the Parliament lights up across the river. The lower terraces are free.

Night. Back in Pest, the ruin bar crawl starts at 21:10 from Erzsébet körút 36. Prefer a quiet drink? Szimpla Kert is free to walk into.

Day 3: Thermal Baths and City Park

Morning. Take the M1 to Széchenyi fürdő and be through the gate by 9:00 for the €39 morning pass (Monday to Thursday). Soak as long as you like.

Afternoon. Walk out into City Park: Vajdahunyad Castle is next door and free, Heroes' Square is at the end of the park, and Andrássy út leads you back towards the centre past the Opera.

Evening. Your last night is the one for a long dinner. Book the best dinner cruise for a 4-course meal on the river, or keep it free and eat in the Jewish Quarter.

If You Only Have 2 Days in Budapest

Keep day 1 exactly as it is: Parliament, Basilica and the night cruise. On day 2, start at Széchenyi before 9:00, take the M1 back to the centre and bus 16 up to Castle Hill for the 14:00 castle walk, then end in the ruin bars. You lose the food tour and the slow afternoon in City Park, and that is all. Bookings: about €130 per person.

Day 4: The Danube Bend

A fourth day is best spent outside the city. The Danube Bend day trip takes you to Visegrád castle above the river's sharp turn and to Szentendre's galleries and lanes, 8 to 9 hours from Batthyány tér for €96. If you would rather stay in town, add Rudas for its rooftop pool, or plan around the December and November events.

Before You Go: Getting In, Where to Stay, What It Costs

  • Getting in from the airport: The 100E express bus costs 2,200 HUF (about €6) and runs to the centre around the clock. All the options are in the airport to city centre guide.
  • Where to stay: Stay in Pest, in District V or VII: every day of this plan starts within walking distance or one metro stop. On a budget, see the best hostels in Budapest.
  • Getting around: Walk inside the centre. For Castle Hill take bus 16, for Széchenyi the M1, and for the river views tram 2, which runs along the Pest embankment.
  • Budapest Card: Whether it pays off depends on your exact plan. Check it against this itinerary with the Budapest Card calculator, which covers all 94 sights.
  • Visiting in winter: Add the Christmas markets to any evening from late November, and plan the night cruise early: it is dark by about 16:00.
  • Longer river cruise: Starting or ending a longer river cruise here? The 15-day Danube and Rhine sailings are compared in Budapest to Amsterdam river cruises. For a shorter trip, the 5 to 8-day Budapest to Vienna river cruises cover the first stretch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 3 days enough for Budapest?

Yes, for a first visit. Three days covers Pest and the Parliament, Buda's Castle Hill, a night cruise, a thermal bath and the ruin bars without rushing. With a fourth day, add a day trip to the Danube Bend.

What is the best order to see Budapest?

Pest first, Buda second, baths last. Day 1 covers the Parliament, the Basilica and a night cruise; day 2 a food tour, Castle Hill at sunset and the ruin bars; day 3 Széchenyi at opening, when the morning pass is €39 instead of €46.

How much does 3 days in Budapest cost?

The bookings in this plan come to about €190 per person: Parliament (€43), the Basilica (€12), a night cruise (€20), a food tour (€62), a castle walk (€12), a pub crawl (€2) and Széchenyi (€39). Food, drinks, hotel and transport are on top.

Can you see Budapest in 2 days?

Yes. Day 1: Parliament, the Basilica and a night cruise. Day 2: Széchenyi before 9:00, the 14:00 Castle Hill walk and the ruin bars in the evening. Bookings come to about €130 per person.

Should I stay in Buda or Pest?

Pest, in District V or VII. It is flatter, has most restaurants and bars, and every day of this itinerary starts within walking distance or one metro stop.

Do I need to book the Parliament in advance?

Yes. You can only go inside on a 45-minute guided tour, and English slots sell out, often days ahead from May to September. Official tickets cost HUF 7,000 for EU/EEA adults and HUF 14,000 for everyone else.
