9 Best Concerts and Shows in Budapest in 2026 (Compared Side by Side)

By Adam Dimitrov, Founder & Budapest Local
9 concerts and shows compared on programme, seats, length, price per hour and dinner
Last updated: Nov 20, 2026

The right concert is the one that fits your evening: an organ concert inside St Stephen's Basilica, Vivaldi or Mozart with a string orchestra, a Hungarian folk dance show, with or without dinner, or dinner with live music on the Danube. This page gets you to that seat in under a minute.

To make the choice easy, I compared all 9 on what really decides it: what you actually hear (organ, strings or the full Mozart Requiem), seat categories, length, price per hour and whether dinner is in the price, checked on each seller's own page. Practical Tips: Seats, Timing and What Costs Extra

Seats. In the Basilica, rows decide the price: VIP row 1, Category I rows 2–6, Category II rows 7–16, Category III rows 17–26. The sound carries to the back, the view does not. St Michael's has free seating, so arrive early if you want the front. St Anna's doors open 15 minutes before the start.

Comfort and timing. Church concerts run 60–70 minutes, or up to 130 minutes for the Basilica organ concert. St Michael's has heated pews as well as more comfortable chairs. At St Anna, latecomers are let in between pieces; at Apostolok, arrive 10–15 minutes before your sitting.

Access. St Michael's, St Anna, the Basilica series (#6) and Apostolok are listed as wheelchair-accessible. The Opera House tour and the Rajkó cellar show are not.

What costs extra. None of these lists transport or hotel pick-up as included. At the dinner shows, Puli & Juhász charges for drinks after the welcome glass, and Apostolok's unlimited drinks exclude spirits and cocktails.

Plan the evening. St Michael's is on Váci utca and St Anna a few steps from it, so you can walk to dinner afterwards; see our food tours if you want a guided eat first. Matthias Church pairs with a Castle Hill walk at sunset. For more evening ideas, see the best things to do in Budapest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best organ concert in Budapest?

For most visitors, the organ concert in St Stephen's Basilica from €26: organ, bass and flute, ending with Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor. For the lowest price and a closer view, St Michael's Church on Váci utca plays the city's oldest working organ from €23, with a screen showing the organist.

How much is a concert in St Stephen's Basilica?

Two series play there. The organ concert starts at €26 with four seat categories by row (VIP row 1 to Category III rows 17–26). The other classical series, including Mozart's Requiem on some dates, starts at €60 with four seating classes.

Can I arrive late to a church concert in Budapest?

At St Anna Church, yes: doors open 15 minutes before the start and latecomers are let in between pieces, so you miss one piece, not the concert. For the dinner shows, Apostolok asks you to arrive 10–15 minutes before your sitting, and the Rajkó evening runs to a fixed clock from 18:30.

Can I tour the Budapest Opera House and hear music?

Yes. The 60-minute guided Opera House tour from €33 ends with a brief live concert on the grand staircase landing. Tours run in English, German, French, Italian and Spanish, and can be rescheduled when there are rehearsals.

Where can I see a Hungarian folk dance show in Budapest?

The Rajkó Folk Ensemble performs a 60-minute show on Király utca from €15, with soup or dinner as options. Puli & Juhász on Dob utca serves a 3-course dinner with live folk music and two 15-minute dance sets for €55.

Which concert is best for people who don't know classical music?

Vivaldi's Four Seasons at St Anna Church, €28 for one hour. An orchestra, violin soloist and soprano play the pieces almost everyone recognises: Vivaldi, Pachelbel's Canon, Bach's Air, Beethoven's 5th.

Are there concerts on a Danube cruise?

Yes, several dinner cruises have live music on board. They are compared separately in our dinner cruises guide.
