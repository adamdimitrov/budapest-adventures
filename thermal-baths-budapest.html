City Park, District XIV • ⏱️ Half Day • M1 Széchenyi fürdő

Széchenyi Thermal Bath

4.332k+ reviews · 3 sites▾
Average of 3 sites, weighted by number of reviews GetYourGuide4.3(29.2k+)→ Tiqets4.4(1.2k+)→ Viator3.8(2.3k+)→Viator’s count includes Tripadvisor reviews. Each site rates its own ticket for this bath.
✓ Personally Tested
From €39
▾

Pros:

  • Outdoor pools all year: An outdoor thermal pool at 36–38 °C, an adventure pool at 32–34 °C and a 26–28 °C swimming pool, open in winter too.
  • Huge choice inside: The listings count 15 indoor pools, plus five saunas from 50 to 90 °C and three steam cabins.
  • Cheap early start: Mon–Thu entry before 9:00 is €39 on GetYourGuide, or 10,500 HUF at the desk.
  • Late Fridays: Open until 22:00 on Fridays, and the Sparty pool party runs on Saturday nights.

Cons:

  • Crowds: It is the most-reviewed bath on GetYourGuide; by late morning the outdoor pools are packed.
  • Online tickets cost more: The weekday locker ticket is €46 on GetYourGuide against 13,200 HUF at the desk, though GetYourGuide lets you cancel for free.
  • Bring your own kit: Towels and flip-flops are not included; the desk sells a towel for 6,900 HUF and slippers for 4,200 HUF.

Booking Széchenyi? See the Széchenyi tickets guide (day ticket from €39, locker vs cabin, Palm House and Beer Spa), including Sparty, the Saturday night pool party.

Summary & Review Overview Overview ▾
❓ Is Széchenyi worth it?
💡 Yes, for a first visit. It is the biggest and most photogenic bath in Budapest. Go early on a weekday to avoid the crowds.

Széchenyi is the bath most visitors picture: three outdoor pools steaming in front of a yellow neo-baroque palace in City Park. It is also the busiest. My best advice from my own visit: arrive between 7:00 and 10:00 on a weekday.

Based on 29,000+ GetYourGuide reviews (4.3 out of 5), visitors love the outdoor pools in winter and the architecture. The most common complaints are crowds later in the day and the price of booking-site tickets.

What visitors say:
“A great way to spend a day in Budapest.” (GetYourGuide, November 2026)
“Go in the morning when there are still few people and the baths are clean.” (GetYourGuide, November 2026)
“Outside pools were especially atmospheric in the rain” (GetYourGuide, November 2026)

Inside the Bath: Pools, Saunas & Changing Rooms From My Visit ▾

Entrance & Tickets

  • Day tickets enter on the swimming-pool side. Booking-site tickets are validated at self-service machines at the entrance, so you skip the cashier queue.

Lockers & Cabins

  • A standard ticket includes a locker in the gender-separated changing area. You get a plastic wristwatch that opens it (3,000 HUF if lost). A private cabin costs 1,000 HUF extra.

Outdoor Pools

  • Three outdoor pools: the thermal pool (36–38 °C), the adventure pool with its whirlpool (32–34 °C) and the swimming pool (26–28 °C, 910 m²).

Indoor Pools

  • The indoor wings hold medicinal pools from 28 to 40 °C and plunge pools at 18 °C and 40 °C.

Saunas & Steam

  • Volcano sauna 70–80 °C, Dante and Phoenix saunas 80–90 °C, two aroma saunas 50–55 °C, three steam cabins 50–60 °C and a salt room at 35–40 °C.

What to Bring

  • Swimsuit, towel and flip-flops. If you forget them, the desk sells a towel (6,900 HUF), slippers (4,200 HUF) and a swim cap (2,100 HUF).

Night Bathing & Sparty

  • Open until 22:00 on Fridays. Sparty, the Saturday night pool party, is sold separately (18+, from €73, non-refundable).

🕌 #2 Best Turkish Bath & Rooftop
Elizabeth Bridge, District I • ⏱️ 3–4 Hours • Tram 19, 41 · Bus 7

Rudas Thermal Bath

4.21.4k+ reviews · GetYourGuide▾
Rating on GetYourGuide GetYourGuide4.2(1.4k+)→
✓ Personally Tested
From €74.99
▾

Pros:

  • Rooftop panorama pool: A 36 °C pool on the roof with views over the Danube; my favourite in the cold months.
  • Real Turkish bath: Six pools from 16 to 42 °C under the Ottoman dome, on separate men-only and women-only days.
  • Night bathing: Friday and Saturday from 22:00 to 3:00 (15,000 HUF, online only).
  • Meal included: The GetYourGuide ticket adds a 3-course meal at Rudas Bistro, and reviewers rate the food highly.

Cons:

  • Confusing layout: Reviewers find it hard to move between the wellness area, the Turkish bath and the rooftop.
  • Crowded at peak times: Some reviewers call the bath overcrowded, especially the rooftop pool.
  • Only a meal package online: No booking site sells a plain entry ticket; the desk price is 12,000 HUF on weekdays.
  • Not for everyone: No children under 14 and currently no access for guests with reduced mobility.
Summary & Review Overview Overview ▾
❓ Is Rudas better than Széchenyi?
💡 For atmosphere, often yes: the Ottoman Turkish bath and the rooftop pool are unique. For a family day out, no: Rudas admits no under-14s.

What makes Rudas unique is the rooftop panorama pool, which looks over the Danube towards Gellért Hill. It is lovely to soak in it in the grim winter months. The other highlight is the Ottoman Turkish bath with its octagonal pool under a stone dome, which has men-only and women-only days.

Based on 1,400+ GetYourGuide reviews (4.2 out of 5), visitors praise the Turkish bath, the views and the food at Rudas Bistro. The recurring complaints are crowds and a confusing layout.

What visitors say:
“Fun experience and beautiful views when you're in the upper pool.” (GetYourGuide, November 2026)
“Brilliant bathhouse, especially the Turkish one with the sandstone columns” (GetYourGuide, October 2026)
“The bath is overcrowded and tourist trap.” (GetYourGuide, October 2026)

Inside the Bath: Pools, Saunas & Changing Rooms From My Visit ▾

Rooftop Panorama Pool

Turkish Bath

Wellness Pools

Saunas & Steam

  • Wellness side: two Finnish saunas (90–100 °C and 60–70 °C), an aroma sauna, a steam cabin and a salt room. Turkish side: three Finnish saunas and a steam room.

Lockers & Cabins

  • A regular ticket includes a locker. A private cabin costs 1,000 HUF extra at the desk.

Drinking Hall

  • The drinking fountain is open Tue and Thu 10:00–14:00. Photo: rudasfurdo.hu.

Night Bathing

  • Friday and Saturday 22:00–3:00. Tickets are sold online only: 15,000 HUF (18,000 HUF on 1 and 2 January 2027).

Food & Drink

  • Rudas Bistro and Rudas Café are inside the bath. The GetYourGuide ticket includes a 3-course meal at the bistro.

Thermal Baths: Know Before You Go

What to Bring and Wear

Bring a swimsuit, a towel and flip-flops. Tickets do not include towels: the city-run baths sell a towel for 6,900 HUF and slippers for 4,200 HUF at the desk (2026 price lists). If you plan to swim laps, bring a swim cap.

When to Visit

All six baths in this guide are open all year. Weekday mornings are the quietest; Széchenyi and Rudas get busy from late morning and at weekends. Palatinus is a summer strand: in winter it closes at 16:00. Bath hours over Christmas are in our December guide, and the best November evenings around the baths are in our November guide.

Lockers and Cabins

A standard ticket includes a locker in a shared changing room. A private cabin costs 1,000 HUF extra at Széchenyi, Rudas, Lukács and Palatinus, and 800 HUF at Dandár.

Age Limits

Rudas, Lukács, Dandár and Veli Bej do not admit children under 14. For a family visit, choose Palatinus.

Water Temperature

Most thermal pools are between 32 and 38 °C. The hottest pools reach 40 to 42 °C, and the cold plunge pools go down to 11 °C at Rudas.

Closed Baths

Gellért has been closed since 1 October 2025 and is expected to reopen in 2028. Király is closed indefinitely. Neither is in this list.

Brief History of Thermal Baths in Budapest

Budapest sits on a network of thermal springs, and people have bathed here since Roman times. During Ottoman rule, Turkish baths were built in Buda; Rudas and Veli Bej still have their Ottoman pools and domes today.

The grand bathhouses came later: Széchenyi, the neo-baroque palace in City Park, opened in 1913. Today most of the city's baths are run by Budapest Gyógyfürdői és Hévizei Zrt., while Veli Bej is run by the Brothers Hospitallers.

Why Visit a Thermal Bath in Budapest?

Relaxation and rejuvenation

Visiting a thermal spa in Budapest is an excellent way to unwind and relax after (or before!) a long day of sightseeing.

The warm waters, saunas, and massage services offered at the baths provide the perfect environment for relaxing your body and mind.

Cultural experience

Thermal spas have been a part of Hungarian culture for centuries, and visiting one is a great way to experience a unique aspect of the local culture.

Each spa has its unique history, architecture, and atmosphere, making it a memorable experience.

The famous baths are busier and pricier than the local ones: a weekday desk ticket is 13,200 HUF at Széchenyi against 3,500 HUF at Dandár. Both are worth a visit; they are simply different experiences.

Health benefits

Although the science is inconclusive, thermal spas are sought for their therapeutic properties, which can help with everything from muscle pain and stiffness to stress and tension.

The warm, mineral-rich waters in the thermal baths have been used for centuries to improve health and wellness. Additionally, many spas offer additional treatments and therapies like massage, hydrotherapy, and sauna that can positively impact overall well-being.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the best thermal bath in Budapest?

Széchenyi for a first visit, Rudas for a Turkish bath and a rooftop pool, Palatinus for families, and Lukács, Dandár or Veli Bej for a quieter, local bath.

Are Gellért and Király open?

No. Gellért has been closed since 1 October 2025 and is expected to reopen in 2028, and Király is closed indefinitely.

How much does a thermal bath cost in Budapest?

At the desk in 2026: from 3,500 HUF at Dandár to 13,200 HUF at Széchenyi on weekdays. Booking-site tickets start at €22 (Dandár) and €39 (Széchenyi, entry before 9:00).

Can children go to the thermal baths?

Only to some. Rudas, Lukács, Dandár and Veli Bej do not admit children under 14. Palatinus has kids' pools and family tickets.

Do you need flip-flops for Budapest baths?

Yes, bring flip-flops, a towel and a swimsuit. Towels are not included in the ticket; the city-run baths sell towels for 6,900 HUF and slippers for 4,200 HUF.

Is there night bathing in Budapest?

Yes. Rudas has night bathing on Friday and Saturday from 22:00 to 3:00, Széchenyi is open until 22:00 on Fridays, and the Sparty pool party at Széchenyi runs on Saturday nights.
