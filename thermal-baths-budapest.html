2026 Thermal Bath Comparison

6 Best Thermal Baths in Budapest in 2026 (4 Personally Tested)

By Adam Dimitrov, Founder & Local Budapest Tester
Budapest Local • 6 Baths Compared Side by Side
Last updated: Nov 20, 2026

The right thermal bath is the one that fits your day: the big outdoor pools at Széchenyi, an Ottoman Turkish bath with a rooftop pool, a local bath without the tourist crowds, or a summer day with the kids on Margaret Island. This page gets you to that bath in under a minute.

To make the choice easy, I bathed in 4 of these 6 myself and compared all 6 on what really matters: pools, changing rooms, crowds and value for money (Gellért and Király are closed, so they are not on the list). Filter by the ones I tested below, or scroll straight to the ranking.

❓ 5 questions travellers ask ▾
Is Széchenyi worth it?
Yes, for a first visit. It is the biggest and most photogenic bath in Budapest. Go early on a weekday to avoid the crowds.
When is it least crowded?
Between 7:00 and 10:00 on a weekday. By late morning the outdoor pools are packed.
Locker or cabin?
A standard ticket includes a locker in the gender-separated changing area, opened with a plastic wristwatch (3,000 HUF if lost). A private cabin costs 1,000 HUF extra.
Are towels included?
No. Bring a swimsuit, towel and flip-flops; the desk sells a towel for 6,900 HUF, slippers for 4,200 HUF and a swim cap for 2,100 HUF.
Can I bathe at night?
It is open until 22:00 on Fridays. Sparty, the Saturday night pool party, is sold separately: 18+, from €73, non-refundable.

❓ 5 questions travellers ask ▾
Is Rudas better than Széchenyi?
For atmosphere, often yes: the Ottoman Turkish bath and the rooftop pool are unique. For a family day out, no: Rudas admits no under-14s.
When are the men-only and women-only days?
Men: Mon, Thu and Fri 6:00–10:45 and all day Wednesday. Women: all day Tuesday. Weekends are mixed.
Can I go at night?
Yes, Friday and Saturday 22:00–3:00. Night tickets are sold online only: 15,000 HUF (18,000 HUF on 1 and 2 January 2027). Night buses 907 and 973 run afterwards.
What does this ticket include?
A day pass plus a 3-course meal at Rudas Bistro, from €74.99. A plain entry ticket is only sold at the desk.
Can I bring children?
No. There are no children under 14, and currently no access for guests with reduced mobility.

❓ 5 questions travellers ask ▾
Is Lukács a good alternative to Széchenyi?
Yes, if you want thermal water without the crowds. It is smaller and plainer, closer to a local bath than a tourist sight.
How hot are the pools?
Indoor medicinal pools at 32, 36 and 40 °C, a 20 °C cold pool, an outdoor adventure pool at 33–35 °C and swimming pools at 22 and 26 °C.
Is it open late?
Until 22:00 every Tuesday. The other days it is open 7:00–19:00.
Is it step-free?
Not right now: the wheelchair lifts are out of order and the private tubs are closed for renovation.
How do I find it?
Frankel Leó út 25-29: tram 4, 6, 17 or 19, bus 9 or 109, or the HÉV to Margit híd, budai hídfő. Veli Bej is on the same street, so do not mix them up.

❓ 5 questions travellers ask ▾
Is Palatinus worth it in winter?
Only for a short visit. In winter it closes at 16:00, and the Finnish sauna is out of service from 14 Nov 2026.
What is there for kids?
A shallow outdoor kids' pool (30–32 °C), an indoor one (32–34 °C) and a 28 °C wave pool; the slides open in summer. Child tickets are for ages 3–14.
Is it real thermal water?
Yes: two thermal pools at 30–32 °C and 33–36 °C plus a 36 °C indoor medicinal pool.
How do I get there?
Bus 26 from Nyugati pályaudvar or Árpád híd stops right outside. In summer the bath also runs its own minibus.
Are the lockers big?
Adult tickets include a locker, but I found them small, so pack light. A cabin ticket costs 1,000 HUF.

Thermal Baths: Know Before You Go

What to Bring and Wear

Bring a swimsuit, a towel and flip-flops. Tickets do not include towels: the city-run baths sell a towel for 6,900 HUF and slippers for 4,200 HUF at the desk (2026 price lists). If you plan to swim laps, bring a swim cap.

When to Visit

All six baths in this guide are open all year. Weekday mornings are the quietest; Széchenyi and Rudas get busy from late morning and at weekends. Palatinus is a summer strand: in winter it closes at 16:00. Bath hours over Christmas are in our December guide, and the best November evenings around the baths are in our November guide.

Lockers and Cabins

A standard ticket includes a locker in a shared changing room. A private cabin costs 1,000 HUF extra at Széchenyi, Rudas, Lukács and Palatinus, and 800 HUF at Dandár.

Age Limits

Rudas, Lukács, Dandár and Veli Bej do not admit children under 14. For a family visit, choose Palatinus.

Water Temperature

Most thermal pools are between 32 and 38 °C. The hottest pools reach 40 to 42 °C, and the cold plunge pools go down to 11 °C at Rudas.

Closed Baths

Gellért has been closed since 1 October 2025 and is expected to reopen in 2028. Király is closed indefinitely. Neither is in this list.

Brief History of Thermal Baths in Budapest

Budapest sits on a network of thermal springs, and people have bathed here since Roman times. During Ottoman rule, Turkish baths were built in Buda; Rudas and Veli Bej still have their Ottoman pools and domes today.

The grand bathhouses came later: Széchenyi, the neo-baroque palace in City Park, opened in 1913. Today most of the city's baths are run by Budapest Gyógyfürdői és Hévizei Zrt., while Veli Bej is run by the Brothers Hospitallers.

Want the thermal water in your hotel?

Two hotels on Margaret Island have thermal pools free for guests: compare the spa and thermal hotels in Budapest.

Why Visit a Thermal Bath in Budapest?

Relaxation and rejuvenation

Visiting a thermal spa in Budapest is an excellent way to unwind and relax after (or before!) a long day of sightseeing.

The warm waters, saunas, and massage services offered at the baths provide the perfect environment for relaxing your body and mind.

Cultural experience

Thermal spas have been a part of Hungarian culture for centuries, and visiting one is a great way to experience a unique aspect of the local culture.

Each spa has its unique history, architecture, and atmosphere, making it a memorable experience.

The famous baths are busier and pricier than the local ones: a weekday desk ticket is 13,200 HUF at Széchenyi against 3,500 HUF at Dandár. Both are worth a visit; they are simply different experiences.

Health benefits

Although the science is inconclusive, thermal spas are sought for their therapeutic properties, which can help with everything from muscle pain and stiffness to stress and tension.

The warm, mineral-rich waters in the thermal baths have been used for centuries to improve health and wellness. Additionally, many spas offer additional treatments and therapies like massage, hydrotherapy, and sauna that can positively impact overall well-being.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the best thermal bath in Budapest?

Széchenyi for a first visit, Rudas for a Turkish bath and a rooftop pool, Palatinus for families, and Lukács, Dandár or Veli Bej for a quieter, local bath.

Are Gellért and Király open?

No. Gellért has been closed since 1 October 2025 and is expected to reopen in 2028, and Király is closed indefinitely.

How much does a thermal bath cost in Budapest?

At the desk in 2026: from 3,500 HUF at Dandár to 13,200 HUF at Széchenyi on weekdays. Booking-site tickets start at €22 (Dandár) and €39 (Széchenyi, entry before 9:00).

Can children go to the thermal baths?

Only to some. Rudas, Lukács, Dandár and Veli Bej do not admit children under 14. Palatinus has kids' pools and family tickets.

Do you need flip-flops for Budapest baths?

Yes, bring flip-flops, a towel and a swimsuit. Towels are not included in the ticket; the city-run baths sell towels for 6,900 HUF and slippers for 4,200 HUF.

Is there night bathing in Budapest?

Yes. Rudas has night bathing on Friday and Saturday from 22:00 to 3:00, Széchenyi is open until 22:00 on Fridays, and the Sparty pool party at Széchenyi runs on Saturday nights.
