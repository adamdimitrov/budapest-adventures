Hungary Tourism Statistics 2026 (How Many Tourists Visit?)

By Adam Dimitrov · Founder & Local Budapest Guide
Compiled from Official Eurostat & KSH Data • 8 Charts with Data Tables • Methodology
Last updated: Nov 18, 2026

Hungary recorded 13.88 million check-ins at tourist accommodation and 33.08 million nights in hotels, guesthouses, rentals and campsites in 2025. Foreign arrivals rose 7.9% and passed their 2019 level for the first time, and international visitors spent €8.16 billion in the country.

A check-in is not a person: the same traveller is counted every time they check in, and Hungarian residents on holiday at home make up more than half of the total. This page covers tourism in the whole of Hungary: how many tourists visit, how long they stay, which months and regions are busiest, where visitors come from and how much they spend. For the capital only, see our Budapest tourism statistics. Every chart has a data table and a link to its official source.

  • Hungary recorded 13,876,111 check-ins at tourist accommodation in 2025, 5.7% more than in 2024 and 3.1% above 2019.
  • Guests spent 33,075,720 nights in Hungarian hotels, guesthouses, rentals and campsites, up 4.0% but still just below 2019 (33,199,961).
  • International guests made 6.60 million arrivals (47.6%) and spent 16.19 million nights (48.9%), both above their 2019 level for the first time.
  • Budapest takes 60.8% of all foreign nights in Hungary. Outside the capital, Western Transdanubia (the spa towns near the Austrian border and the west end of Lake Balaton) is the most visited region.
  • Germany is the largest foreign market with 1.90 million nights, followed by Czechia (1.11 million) and the United Kingdom (1.08 million).
  • August is the peak month with 4.86 million nights, 2.9 times as many as January.
  • International visitors spent €8.16 billion in Hungary in 2025, 9.5% more than in 2024 (not adjusted for inflation).
  • Hungary ranks 17th in the EU for nights spent by foreign guests, between Denmark and Bulgaria.

In Hungary the split between domestic and international guests is close to even: 52.4% of arrivals were Hungarian residents. Foreign arrivals were hit harder by the pandemic, falling to 1.37 million in 2020, and only passed their 2019 level in 2025 (+7.0%).

An arrival is a check-in at registered accommodation, not a person. Stays with friends or family and day trips, for example shopping or spa visits from neighbouring countries, are not counted.

Hungary ranks 17th in the EU for nights spent by foreign guests in tourist accommodation, with 16.19 million in 2025, 1.1% of the EU total. Among its neighbours, Austria and Croatia record five to six times as many.

Sources and methodology

  • Arrivals, nights and length of stay: Eurostat, tour_occ_arnat (arrivals) and tour_occ_ninat (nights), NACE codes I551 to I553: hotels, holiday and other short-stay accommodation, and campsites. Length of stay is nights divided by arrivals. Data accessed 14 November 2026 (dataset updated 12 October 2026).
  • Months: Eurostat tour_occ_nim, 2025. Monthly figures are published separately from the annual series and can differ from the annual total by a small rounding or revision margin.
  • Countries of origin: Eurostat tour_occ_ninraw, nights by country of residence of the guest, 2024 and 2025.
  • Regions: Eurostat tour_occ_nin2, nights by NUTS region, 2025.
  • Spending: Eurostat bop_its6_det, balance of payments item SD (travel), credit, partner: rest of the world, EUR million. This is what non-residents spend in the country on goods and services during trips, including business travel and students, but excluding international passenger transport.
  • What the data does not cover: stays with friends and family, unregistered private rentals and day trips are not in the accommodation statistics. An arrival is a check-in, so one traveller staying at two hotels on the same trip counts twice. Eurostat publishes figures supplied by the Hungarian Central Statistical Office (KSH), which draws on the NTAK accommodation registry. Spending figures come from the Hungarian National Bank (MNB) via Eurostat.
