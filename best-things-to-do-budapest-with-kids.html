9 Best Things to Do in Budapest with Kids in 2026 (Compared Side by Side)

By Adam Dimitrov, Founder & Budapest Local
9 family activities compared on age rules, length, price per hour, indoor vs outdoor and location
Last updated: Nov 16, 2026

Book the chimney cake workshop (€20 per person, 65 minutes) if you only do one paid family activity: kids roll and bake their own kürtőskalács, pick a topping, eat it warm and leave with a certificate, and one adult covers up to four children. If it's raining, the Pinball Museum (€15.50) lets kids play up to 150 machines until midnight.

What decides the rest is age and weather. The Cat Museum turns away under-8s and strollers, the Budapest Eye needs an adult with anyone under 14, and only one route states it is pushchair-friendly. Five of the nine are fully indoors, so a wet day is covered.

Below are 7 tickets and workshops from €12.60 to €37 and 2 guided family walks, compared on age fit, length, price per hour, indoor vs outdoor and location. Filter by rainy day, outdoor, teens, family tours or under €25.

🏆 #1 Best Overall
Chimney cake workshop studio • ⏱️ 65 minutes • 📍 Ipar u. 11, District IX

Budapest: Traditional Chimney Cake (kürtőskalács) Workshop

4.9(1.7k+)
From €20
▾

Pros:

  • Hands-on from start to finish, dough already risen
  • Toppings, apron, coffee or tea and a certificate included
  • Short enough for younger attention spans
  • Indoor, so it works on a wet or freezing day
  • One adult can supervise up to four children

Cons:

  • 15 minutes from the centre by public transport
  • Not safe for several common allergies
  • Instruction in English
  • Pre-made dough, so kids don't mix it themselves
📝 Review & Quick Facts Overview ▾

This is the best all-round family pick on the list because it is short, hands-on and edible. For about €20 per person each child rolls, wraps and bakes a kürtőskalács on a wooden spindle, picks a topping (cinnamon sugar, vanilla, cocoa or walnut), then sits down to eat it, with coffee or tea included. A certificate at the end gives kids something to show off.

At 65 minutes (45 baking, 20 eating) it costs about €19 an hour, which is cheaper per hour than a guided family tour and far more active than a museum ticket. The dough is pre-prepared because it needs around 75 minutes to rise, so nobody waits around, but kids also don't mix it themselves.

Two catches. The studio at Ipar u. 11 is about 15 minutes from the centre by public transport, so build it into a half day rather than squeezing it between sights. And the allergen warning is strict: wheat, milk, nuts, orange and coconut are all in the room. For more cooking options, including full Hungarian meals, see our Budapest cooking classes comparison.

Cats nap during the day and the museum says plainly it cannot predict when they will be playful, so go in expecting a calm visit rather than a show. At Vadász utca 26 it sits in District V, like the Retro Interactive Museum on Október 6. utca.

Which One Should You Book?

  • One activity, mixed ages: the chimney cake workshop. Everyone bakes and eats in 65 minutes, and one adult can supervise up to four kids.
  • It's raining: five of the nine are fully indoors. Go for the Pinball Museum for a long session, or the Retro Museum and Cat Museum, which are both in District V and easy to pair.
  • Toddler or pushchair: skip the Cat Museum (no under-8s, no strollers). The mini-sculpture walk is the only option that states a pushchair-friendly route, if Russian works for you.
  • Teenagers: the Retro Museum's Lada simulator, the Selfie Museum's sugar-pearl pool and pinball until midnight are the three strongest teen picks.
  • Evening plans: the Budapest Eye, open until 23:00 (midnight on Fridays and Saturdays), or pinball until midnight. For a story-themed light walk, book Lumina.
  • First day, want the big sights covered: the private family city tour does Parliament, the basilica and the Buda hilltop in 3 hours with playground breaks.
  • Tight budget: the Cat Museum at €12.60 for kids 8 and up, or the Selfie Museum at €15.07.

Practical Tips for Budapest with Kids

Group the stops by area. The Budapest Eye and the mini-sculpture walk both start on Erzsébet tér, and the family city tour meets at Gerbeaud on Vörösmarty tér, also in District V. The Cat Museum (Vadász utca) and Retro Museum (Október 6. utca) are both in District V. The chimney cake studio (District IX) is about 15 minutes from the centre by public transport and the Pinball Museum is in District XIII, so plan each as its own outing.

What to bring: an ID card (a copy is fine) for the Budapest Eye; a fully charged smartphone for the AR parts of the mini-sculpture walk; warm clothing, comfortable shoes and a card (it's cashless) for Lumina; socks without holes for the Cat Museum, where shoes come off.

What costs extra: museum admissions on the family city tour, food and drinks on the mini-sculpture walk, and the Retro Museum's bistro. The chimney cake workshop already includes the cake, toppings and coffee or tea.

Not on this list: Budapest Zoo, the Tropicarium and the Children's Railway are outside this comparison, which sticks to bookable tickets, workshops and tours.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best thing to do in Budapest with kids?

The chimney cake workshop: for about €20 per person, kids bake their own kürtőskalács in 65 minutes, eat it with toppings and take home a certificate. One adult is required for every 4 children.

What can you do with kids in Budapest when it rains?

Five options are fully indoors: the Pinball Museum (€15.50, play until midnight with re-entry), the chimney cake workshop (€20.29), the Cat Museum (€12.60, ages 8+), the Retro Interactive Museum (about €16) and the Selfie Museum (€15.07).

How much are Budapest Eye tickets?

The skip-the-line ticket is €37.05 per person for a ride of about 8 minutes with at least 3 rotations. A private cabin is only guaranteed with 4 tickets, about €148 for four. It opens at 11:00 every day and closes at 23:00, or midnight on Fridays and Saturdays. Under 14s must be accompanied.

How old do kids have to be for the Cat Museum Budapest?

At least 8. Strollers and baby carriages are not allowed either, shoes come off at the entrance (slippers are provided) and hands must be disinfected before petting the cats.

Can kids play the machines at the Budapest Pinball Museum?

Yes. The ticket covers play on up to 150 machines, from vintage tables to modern ones, on everything except Hercules, the world's biggest pinball machine. A bracelet lets you leave and come back the same night, and it stays open until midnight.

Are there guided tours of Budapest for families?

Two are compared here: a private 3-hour family city tour in English (€75 per person, playground stops, one review so far) and a 2-hour mini-sculpture treasure hunt in Russian (from €185, pushchair-friendly route, new listing).

Is Lumina Light Park suitable for small children?

No minimum age is stated for the 1.5 km Alice in Wonderland trail. The listing asks you to bring warm clothing and comfortable shoes, and it is not suitable for people with epilepsy.
